Cyber
Mindsets
Mindsets function as both a compass and a barometer. They help learners
approach new experiences with the right frame of mind to maximize both
effectiveness and psychological well-being during difficult work. Mindsets also
provide a way to evaluate decisions and actions to ensure they stay true to
positive outcomes. Most people are not born with these mindsets, they are
developable skills if approached with intention.
Passion 	“The future belongs to those who believe in the beauty of their
dreams.”
Eleanor Roosevelt
Cybersecurity is a field that never stops changing and is constantly providing practitioners
new challenges. In order to take on evolving challenges, one must have a passion for
learning and a passion for problem solving. Taking on challenges with optimism and
passion, is the embracing of possibility, the idea that even if we don’t know the answer, it’s
out there and we can find it.
The world of cybersecurity is broad, limitless, and evolving. Students who develop a passion
for this work will not only be able to have endless opportunities available to them, but will
have the ability to create their own.
Curiosity 	“There’s really no secret to our approach. We keep moving forward,
opening new doors and doing new things, because we are curious.”
Walt Disney
The world is a complex place and those that are curious are the most likely to find success.
This idea is especially true in the world of cybersecurity. A field where curiosity, asking
“what if”, is often the path to exploits, intrusions, bugs, and vulnerabilities. When asked what
skill is most important for leaders, Michael Dell, CEO of Dell Inc., replied, “I would place my
bet on curiosity.” Get out there and be curious.
Performance “I hear and I forget. I see and I remember. I do and I understand.”
Confucius
Performance is the concept of doing and is not limited to only when a person is ready. In
the world of cybersecurity, the majority of learning happens through doing real work, getting
one's “hands dirty”. Learning-by-doing is rooted in the fact that failure, and learning from it,
is an incredibly powerful tool for learning. We must embrace a “bias towards action”, and do
things that are “safe enough to try.” Embracing the performance mindset is the belief that
application of knowledge is critical and only developed by doing, failing, experimenting,
reflecting and ultimately learning.

Paradigm Cyber Ventures: Cyber Competitions Guide
Cybersecurity competitions are gamified, interactive events designed to challenge students to solve real-world
technical problems, ranging from patching vulnerabilities in a compromised operating system to deciphering
cryptographic puzzles. Integrating these competitions into a cybersecurity curriculum transforms passive learning
into an active, hands-on experience that mirrors the high-stakes environment of the digital workforce. Ultimately,
cyber competitions bridge the gap between the classroom and the industry, injecting a powerful element of
excitement that engages diverse student populations and helps cultivate the next generation of digital defenders.
UNDERSTANDING THE FORMATS
Before diving into specific competitions, it helps to understand the two primary styles of cyber events:
● Capture the Flag (CTF): Students solve standalone puzzles (cryptography, web vulnerabilities, reverse engineering) to find a text string
(the "flag") and submit it for points. These are highly accessible and usually run entirely in a web browser.
● Blue Team / Cyber Defense: Students are given a simulated, compromised corporate or government network (virtual machines). Their
job is to find vulnerabilities, fix them, secure the system, and maintain services while keeping "hackers" out. These require more computing
power and preparation.

How do CTFs work?
CHALLENGES ARE POSTED
Challenges can be puzzles, hidden files, web tasks, or
other problem-solving activities. There are “Learn”
challenges for each unit of our course and additional
“Play” challenges you can explore on your own!
LOOK FOR CLUES
Clues may be hidden in text, images, code, or
interactives. Sometimes the challenge gives hints about
where to look or what tool to use.
SOLVE THE CHALLENGE
Use your skills and tools to uncover the solution. This
could involve analyzing files, decoding messages, or
exploring networks.
8
CAPTURE THE FLAG
A “flag” is a secret piece of information that proves
you solved the challenge. You will use the same
format for flags each time!
● Format: flag{answer}
● If there are any spaces in the flag, you should
replace them with an underscore. Ex.
flag{the_answer}
SUBMIT YOUR FLAG
Enter the flag into the CTF platform (Centra) to earn
points or record completion.
● Note: Once the timer starts for a challenge, it will
not stop until you have solved the challenge!

How does Scoring work?
● Each challenge has a certain number of points you get if you solve it. This
will be at the top of each challenge.
● There are three levels of difficulty, each with different point values.
● You can increase your points by solving the challenge in your first three
attempts.
● Hints: Each challenge contains a hint if needed. Hints cost you
one attempt.
● Learn Button: Each challenge has a “Learn” button that allows you to learn
material associated with the challenge. Learn buttons
do not affect your
score.
