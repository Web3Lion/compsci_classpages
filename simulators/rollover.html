An 8-bit counter, ticking like an odometer — each wheel rolls and carries into the next. Four modes: plain counting, addition & subtraction, negative numbers, and binary fractions.

Each wheel only ever shows 0 or 1. When a wheel rolls from 1 back to 0, it carries — nudging the wheel to its left up by one, same as a car odometer's 9 rolling to 0. Overflow happens once all 8 wheels max out at 1111 1111 (255) and the next tick has nowhere left to carry to.

Unsigned 8-bit math: values 0–255 only. Adding past 255 overflows and wraps around; subtracting past 0 borrows and wraps around too — exactly like the counter running past 255 back to 0.

Two's complement: to write a negative number in binary, take its positive magnitude, invert every bit, then add 1. The leftmost wheel (highlighted) is the sign bit — 1 means negative. This is why an 8-bit signed byte only reaches 127 positive but −128 negative.

Split the same 8 wheels at a binary point: the left 4 are whole-number bits (8, 4, 2, 1), the right 4 are fraction bits, each worth half the one before it (1/2, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16). The smallest step this register can take is 1/16 = 0.0625.
