A message doesn't travel the internet as one piece — it's split into packets that can each take a different path and get reassembled at the destination. Send a message, then click a relay to take it offline and resend. Watch packets reroute around the failure — that's fault tolerance. Each packet only knows its NEXT hop, not the whole route, so a packet that hits a dead relay travels as far as it can and gets dropped right there, not silently lost at the source.

Real routing is hop-by-hop: each relay only knows which of its own neighbors gets a packet closer to the destination, not the full path in advance. A packet keeps moving forward through online relays until it either reaches the server or lands on a relay with no working next hop — that's where it's dropped, marked with a ✗ right at that node. Redundant paths (multiple relays, multiple links) are what let most packets route around a single failure; enough failures and even hop-by-hop forwarding runs out of road.
