If IPv4's whole address space were one grain of sand, IPv6's would be bigger than every grain of sand on every beach on Earth.
WHY THE SWITCH WAS NECESSARY
THE PROBLEM
IPv4 was designed in 1981, when nobody expected billions of phones, laptops, and smart devices to each need their own address. 4.3 billion sounded infinite \u2014 it wasn't.
THE WORKAROUND
NAT (Network Address Translation) lets many devices on one home/school network share a single public IPv4 address \u2014 it stretched IPv4's life by years, but it's a patch, not a fix.
THE REAL FIX
IPv6 quadruples the bit length (32 \u2192 128), and because address space grows exponentially with bits, that's not 4x more addresses \u2014 it's billions of billions of times more.
WHY NOT JUST ADD A FEW BITS?
Designers wanted headroom that would never run out again \u2014 128 bits leaves room for every device, every network, and every future use case without ever repeating this crisis.
