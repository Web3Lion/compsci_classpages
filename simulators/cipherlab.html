PRACTICE SUMMARY
CAESAR CIPHER

A Caesar cipher shifts every letter of a message forward through the alphabet by the same fixed number of places — the "key". To decode, shift backward by that same amount. It's named for Julius Caesar, who reportedly used a shift of 3 to protect military messages.

3
VIGENÈRE CIPHER

A Vigenère cipher uses a repeating keyword instead of one fixed shift. Each letter of the keyword picks a different shift amount (A=0, B=1…Z=25), so the same plaintext letter can encrypt differently depending on where it falls under the keyword.

With keyword DOG: D is the 3rd letter after A (shift 3), O is the 14th (shift 14), G is the 6th (shift 6). Those three shifts — 3, 14, 6 — then repeat for every letter of the message.
