🔍 What just happened: the rule never saw a "Group A/B" field — it only learned from patterns in the training examples (which features tended to show up in past approvals). Because the training sample itself was skewed toward one group, the rule picked up on features correlated with that group and carried the imbalance forward, even though it looks "neutral" on paper. This is how real-world bias creeps into automated systems: not through explicit discrimination in the code, but through skewed historical data.
